The work Jagrutiben described

RUTVA asked for a hierarchy the partnership can operate from Anand, and for a user to open that same hierarchy on his own domain. The short names in this paper are RUTVA’s: SD, MD, DT, RT.

01 · One desk

Services share a ledger

POS, QR, BBPS, recharge, and payout use the same users, wallets, and reports. A retailer is not sent to a second product to finish the day.

02 · One line

SD, then MD, then DT, then RT

Each seat appoints and funds the seat under it. Schemes and reports stop at that downline. They do not wander across the tree.

03 · White label

His domain, his people

A user adds SD, MD, DT, and RT on an address he owns. Those users get the full service menu, under his name and logo.

A user opens the system on his own domain

White label, in the words this paper is using for RUTVA, means a user of the portal is not a guest on RUTVA’s address. He receives the product on a domain he provides. On that domain he builds a line, and only in this order: Super-Distributor, Master Distributor, Distributor, Retailer. The people he adds are able to use the services. They are not given a reduced menu because they sit on his brand.

His name and logo are what the browser shows. Wallets, schemes, and reports for that line stay inside that downline. RUTVA still runs the estate from the admin seats. One white-label user does not read another user’s books.

On that domain, every seat in the line can use:

BBPS Recharge Payout POS QR
  • The user creates SD, MD, DT, and RT accounts himself.
  • The order stays SD → MD → DT → RT. A retailer is not parked above a distributor.
  • Domain name and DNS come from that user. Shah Works points the portal at the name once DNS is in hand.
  • UAT for a white-label domain happens before that address is treated as live.

His line

  1. SD Super-Distributor. First person he appoints. Can fund the MD seats and run the full menu.
  2. MD Master Distributor. Appointed by SD. Funds distributors. Same services on his own wallet.
  3. DT Distributor. Appointed by MD. Opens retailers, moves funds to them, and works the same menu.
  4. RT Retailer. The counter. BBPS, recharge, payout, POS, QR, wallet, and a receipt.

RUTVA MULTISERVICES LLP Seats and services

What each seat can actually do

The same menu sits on the RUTVA portal and on a white-label domain. What changes is who appointed the user, and whose logo is on the screen.

Point of sale

POS

The life of a machine, from the carton to the day’s settlement.

  • Stock list, including a bulk upload of machines
  • Assign a device to a merchant or a retailer
  • Pay, refund, and void from the bridge
  • Rental plans that debit on their own schedule
  • Card, UPI, BharatQR, and cash
  • Last-seen health for each device
  • Commission carried into a T+1 settlement

QR acceptance

QR

A merchant collects without leaving the portal that holds the wallet.

  • Static QR tied to the merchant VPA
  • Dynamic QR locked to one billed amount
  • Confirmation when the payment lands
  • That merchant’s QR history
  • Settlement and a reconciliation view
  • A QR sheet in the merchant’s brand
  • Duplicates and timeouts do not double-charge

Super-Distributor

SD

The top of RUTVA’s selling line, under admin. The SD wallet is a working wallet, not a viewing seat.

  • BBPS, mobile recharge, DTH, and payout
  • POS and QR on the SD login
  • Open and manage Master Distributors
  • Push funds down to an MD
  • Turn the same services on for the network
  • Schemes, limits, and the full tree

Master Distributor

MD

Sees only the distributors under his SD. Runs the service menu on the MD wallet.

  • BBPS, recharge, and payout on the MD wallet
  • POS and QR on the MD login
  • Open and manage Distributors
  • Fund those wallets and read balances
  • Switch services on for the people below
  • Schemes, limits, and downline reports

Distributor

DT

Builds the retailer book, and also transacts on his own wallet.

  • BBPS, recharge, and payout on the DT wallet
  • POS and QR on the DT login
  • Open retailers and take KYC
  • Top up a retailer and transfer funds
  • Turn the same services on for retailers
  • Earnings, wallets, and settlement for that book

Retailer

RT

The counter. Daily volume stays here, with a receipt and a dispute path.

  • BBPS, recharge, payout, POS, and QR
  • Wallet balance as it moves
  • History that can issue a receipt
  • Mini-statement and balance enquiry
  • File a dispute and follow it
  • SMS or in-app alert on a payment
  • Profile and KYC the retailer can update

Sub-admin

OPS

RUTVA’s office staff work without holding the super-admin key.

  • Rights split across wallets, MDR, and reports
  • A department sees only its own work
  • User management inside that fence
  • An activity log of what was done
  • Reversal and dispute rights by role
  • Maker and checker on sensitive moves
  • Reports limited to the team that needs them

Where the line sits

MAP

Admin is above the selling line. The selling line is the same shape on RUTVA’s portal and on a white-label domain.

  • Super Admin, then Sub-admin
  • SD under admin
  • MD reports to SD
  • DT reports to MD
  • RT reports to DT
  • POS, QR, BBPS, recharge, payout, ledger, and alerts sit under every seat
  • Staging never shares a wallet with production

Four reviews, written for this build

Mr. Patel, or a person he names, sees working software at each review. Shah Works does not open the next stretch until that review is accepted. A short weekly call, or a recorded walkthrough, sits between the reviews so a blocked credential does not wait until week eight.

Weeks 1–2
Review one

Foundation

Project setup, the data model, sign-in, super-admin and sub-admin, user management, portal settings, and the wallet ledger underneath.

Accepted when: a role can sign in on staging and the ledger skeleton holds an entry.

Weeks 3–4
Review two

The line, including white label

SD, MD, DT, and RT screens. Fund transfers. Schemes. Reports that follow the tree. A user can be placed on his domain and can add his own SD, MD, DT, and RT.

Accepted when: a downline can be created, funds can move, reports respect the role, and a white-label domain can be signed into.

Weeks 5–6
Review three

The service menu

POS with rental, QR acceptance, and BBPS, recharge, and payout on SD, MD, DT, and RT — on RUTVA’s portal and on a white-label domain.

Accepted when: sandbox transactions settle, and a device or QR can be assigned.

Weeks 7–8
Review four

Handover

End-to-end checks, a security pass, report finish, UAT with RUTVA’s side, production on the agreed cloud, the white-label domain cut over, two training sessions, and the written handover.

Accepted when: UAT is signed, the production address is live, the white-label domain resolves, and admin access has been handed across.
